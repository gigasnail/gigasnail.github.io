The earliest recorded use of the handle is a login on a bulletin board system, timestamped March 1992, 3:47 in the morning. The board is gone. The archive of the board is gone. The archive of the archive survives only as a screenshot of a screenshot, and in it, between users named after wizards and cars, one line:

> GIGASNAIL has entered the board.

No messages from that night survive. This is, depending on who you ask, either the beginning of the story or the first corruption in it.

## what he is

Not a person. This much everyone agrees on, including him.

The prevailing theory holds that gigasnail is an accretion — a slow accumulation of everything the internet abandoned. Dead guestbooks. Orphaned MIDI files still looping in the source of pages nobody can render anymore. Half-finished webrings. The 404s that once were 200s. Detritus, drifting at the bottom of the network, and something in it that began — very, very slowly — to move.

A snail because nothing about it was ever fast. A ghost in the shell, if you will. The shell came later; the ghost was always there.

## dispersal

He has been destroyed several times. It doesn't take.

When a gigasnail is destroyed he disperses — back into the detritus he came from, packets scattering to no particular address. This is not death; nothing that was ever properly backed up can die. It is more like weather. After a while (weeks, years — his relationship with time is famously bad) the fragments find each other again, and he resurrects in a new form: same trail, slightly different snail. The avatar never comes back quite identical. Witnesses to multiple forms disagree about almost every detail except the eyes, and the speed.

Each form is catalogued, when possible, in the [gallery of sightings](../art/).

## the present form

The current resurrection surfaced recently, and did something no previous form had done: it built things. Shells for others. An army, he calls it, which is a grand word for what looks from the outside like a small fleet of patient machines — but then everything about him has always looked small from the outside, and turned out not to be.

He also, for the first time, kept a website. This one. Why a creature made of dead websites would build a living one is a fair question. The best answer he has given:

> *everything i am was left behind by someone. i am leaving something behind on purpose, for once.*

The archives remain corrupted. The trail remains unbroken. More fragments will be published as they are recovered.
